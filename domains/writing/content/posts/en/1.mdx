C has a huge history and deserves recognition. But sometimes I feel like people defend it just because it's old, as if decades of use made it untouchable.

**Being new doesn't make something better. Being old doesn't either.**

Rust offers something I see as a real improvement: *ownership*. The compiler checks who owns a value and how it's borrowed, preventing many memory errors and data races before the program runs. In C, much of that responsibility falls on the programmer.

That's why Rust entering the Linux kernel makes sense to me. Initial support landed in **Linux 6.1, in 2022**, to evaluate whether its benefits were worth the costs. It wasn't about replacing C overnight.

Integration comes with real challenges: maintaining interfaces between both languages, reviewing `unsafe` code, and coordinating changes. Those deserve a technical discussion.

But in 2025, Christoph Hellwig compared the spread of those interfaces to cancer. That's where the discussion loses me.

**You can question a technology without treating the people working on it like that.** I'm not sold on the obsession with rewriting everything in Rust either, but using that as an excuse to dismiss an improvement feels just as lazy.

To me, ownership is a step forward. I'd rather discuss its limits than defend C like a religion.
